How to use these papers

A good routine: sit a module timed in ESAT Drill (27 questions, 40 minutes, no calculator; Sets C to F are full sittings of all three modules most engineering and physics applicants take), then go through every question with its worked solution, including the ones you got right. Use topic practice to drill whatever cost you the most marks.

How the ESAT works

The Engineering and Science Admissions Test is set by UAT-UK and used by Cambridge, Oxford, Imperial and UCL for engineering, physics, natural sciences and related courses. From 2026 (for 2027 entry) Oxford uses it too, for courses including Engineering Science and Physics. Check each course page for the modules it needs, and the official ESAT page for dates and rules.

5 modulesMathematics 1, Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Mathematics 2. Everyone takes Mathematics 1; most courses add two more.
27 in 4027 multiple-choice questions in 40 minutes per module: under 90 seconds a question.
No calculatorArithmetic is by hand, so questions reward spotting the structure over grinding.
1 to 9Each module is scored on a 1 to 9 scale. Wrong answers lose nothing, so never leave a blank.

Mathematics 1 covers number, ratio, algebra, geometry, statistics and probability, at GCSE Higher and a little beyond. Physics covers electricity, magnetism, mechanics, thermal physics, matter, waves and radioactivity at GCSE level, applied in unfamiliar contexts; the mocks take g = 10 N kg−1. Mathematics 2 covers A-level pure: algebra and functions, sequences and series, coordinate geometry, trigonometry, exponentials and logarithms, differentiation, integration and graphs.
