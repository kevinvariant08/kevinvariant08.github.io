Mock interview simulators

Oxbridge maths interviews are twenty-five minutes with one problem, delivered in parts you cannot see in advance, with a tutor who probes every step and gives ground only when you ask. These simulators follow the same format. Each problem is original, starts with a part most candidates can do and ends with one few can, and comes with a mark scheme, a scored debrief and sample performances scoring 0 and 10, all locked until you finish.

How to run the interview

  • Read the setup aloud. Tell the candidate to show the next part only when the current one is settled, so they never see a part early.
  • Give a hint only when the candidate asks, or after a long silence with no progress. Start with the gentlest one and tick it as you give it.
  • Never give an answer or confirm a number that has not been justified. Ask "why?" and "how do you know?" instead.
  • Let silences sit. A pause is thinking, not a request for help.
  • Stay polite and neutral. Do not praise or reassure, and do not say whether an answer is right.
  • If the candidate is wrong, ask a question that exposes the error, such as a special case to check. Under each part, the common mistakes come with the question to ask.
  • Settle a part only when every point in "What a full answer must contain" has been said or written. Tick the points as they come; a right answer with a point missing gets one of the follow-up questions.
  • Push a strong candidate into the deeper directions, one at a time, once the parts are done.
