Measurements

Use the measure tools.

A relation listed as holding is exact, not just close: it is re-checked to about one part in ten million in twelve randomly nudged copies of the figure, and has to hold in most of them. One that does not is an accident of placement, which tells you what to construct next. Lengths between any two named points are compared too, so relations such as BX = AX + CX appear even when no segment is drawn. Relations that hold by construction are not listed. Similar triangles are found among all named points, drawn or not, and grouped into chains with matching vertex order. Suggest constructions tries every natural extra point or line (feet, midpoints, circumcentres, orthocentres, reflections, intersections, second intersections, antipodes) and lists those that create exact relations, ranked for what you are trying to prove. A construction that lands on a point already in the figure is usually the key step. Hints, when switched on, go further: choose a statement and the page searches for a proof by chasing angles and lengths from what the construction says (never from the measurements), trying one extra point at a time if the figure as drawn is not enough. The proof is then revealed in four layers: a nudge, the construction, the key steps, and an outline with the angles behind each step.
