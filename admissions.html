Admissions

Maths admissions preparation

The TMUA, ESAT and Oxbridge interviews test much the same skills as competitions, under tighter time limits. This page sets out an order of preparation for each, using the papers and tools on this site. Test dates and registration deadlines are in the calendar.

TMUA: a plan in five stages

Two papers, 75 minutes each, 20 multiple-choice questions per paper, no calculator. Paper 1 tests applying A-level maths; Paper 2 tests mathematical reasoning and logic. Allow eight to ten weeks; compress the middle stages if you have less time.

  1. Know the test (week 1)

    Read the official specification and sit one official past paper cold, untimed, to see where you stand. Learn how the site's difficulty ratings work.

  2. Build the toolkit (weeks 1 to 3)

    Work through the technique index, which sorts every question by the technique that solves it, and use Smart Practice, which picks questions to suit your level. For Paper 2 logic and for stronger algebra, the Pathways units Polynomials and Vieta, Inequalities and Writing olympiad solutions are also useful.

  3. Timed papers, in order (weeks 3 to 7)

    Sit one paper at a time in Drill under exam timing. Take the free route in order: Set A, then D, C and B. Then the locked sets from gentlest to hardest: E, F, J, H, I, G, K, L (how unlocking works).

  4. Close the gaps (throughout)

    Every wrong answer goes into Review mistakes, which brings it back after 1, 3 and 7 days. My progress shows your accuracy by topic, your timing, the traps you fall for and whether your confidence matches your results.

  5. Final fortnight

    Full papers back to back under exam conditions, mixing official past papers with the hardest sets here. Practise your guessing policy: there is no negative marking, so never leave a question blank.

ESAT: Mathematics 1, Physics and Mathematics 2

Each module is 27 multiple-choice questions in 40 minutes, no calculator. The mocks here are deliberately harder and longer than the real thing.

  1. Learn by topic

    Use topic practice in ESAT Drill to go through each area of the specification, with a worked solution after every question.

  2. Timed modules, gentlest first

    Set A, then Sets C, D and E, then Set F, with Set B last as the hardest. Sit every module you will take in the real test, at 40 minutes each.

  3. Speed and review

    Go through every solution, including the ones you got right; the faster route is often in the solution. Re-sit topics that cost you most.

Interviews

An Oxbridge maths interview is usually one unfamiliar problem, delivered in parts, with a tutor who wants to hear you think. Competition problems are good preparation, because they are unfamiliar by design.

  1. Think out loud on hard problems

    Work through Pathways Level 4 units, explaining each step aloud as if to a tutor. The worked examples model the kind of reasoning interviewers want to hear.

  2. Sit mock interviews

    The mock interview simulators run a 25-minute problem in parts, with an interviewer that asks you to justify claims and nudges only when you ask, then a scored debrief.

  3. Broaden your toolkit

    The HU Collection problem sets on the olympiad page practise invariants, symmetry and building an argument from scratch, which interview problems often need.

STEP and beyond

STEP rewards the same skills as olympiad algebra and number theory: long, careful arguments. The Pathways Level 4 units on functional equations, inequalities and Diophantine equations are good preparation, alongside the free STEP Support Programme from the University of Cambridge.
