What the book covers

Many students meet olympiad geometry as a pile of intersections and auxiliary points that seem to appear from nowhere in the official solutions. Beyond the Incenter is written to show where those constructions come from. It starts from the triangle's five centres and builds up through radical axes and projective geometry to spiral similarity, proving each tool before using it on competition problems.

It is aimed at students who know A-level or high school mathematics and want to reach BMO, EGMO or IMO standard in geometry. You can read a full chapter, on cross ratios, before buying.

Why I wrote it

The UKMT markers' reports for the last decade say the same thing year after year: geometry questions are "one of the least attempted by candidates", and too many answers give "the algebraic proof with no reference to the geometrical justifications."

Part of the reason is that school syllabuses now teach very little classical geometry, so in a competition most students fall back on coordinates or trigonometry. As the reports point out, that usually means pages of algebra and a good chance of an arithmetic slip. I see the same thing at my own school: out of more than 1,000 students, I know of three who have met Menelaus's theorem and two who can use a complete quadrilateral.

There are excellent books for students already at national training camp level, and much less for the step from A-level up to it. This book is my attempt at that step: radical axes, projective geometry and spiral similarity taught as methods you can choose deliberately, so that a hard configuration has a plan of attack.
