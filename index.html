Problem 3 of six from the unit Power of a point and cyclic quadrilaterals, exactly as it appears there.

Problem 3★★★★

In a convex quadrilateral ABCD the diagonals meet at P, with AP = 4, PC = 9, BP = 6 and PD = 6. Given that AB = 5, find CD.

Hint. Compare PA·PC with PB·PD. What does that tell you about the four vertices?

Answer: 15/2

We have PA·PC = 36 = PB·PD, and P lies inside both diagonals, so by the converse of the intersecting chords theorem ABCD is cyclic. Then ∠BAC = ∠BDC (angles in the same segment), that is ∠PAB = ∠PDC, and ∠APB = ∠DPC (vertically opposite). So triangles APB and DPC are similar, with A, P, B corresponding to D, P, C. The ratio of similarity is DP/AP = 6/4 = 3/2, so CD = (3/2)·AB = 15/2.

As a check, CP/BP should also equal 3/2, and indeed 9/6 = 3/2. In fact that check is a proof on its own: AP/DP = BP/CP with the vertically opposite angles gives the similarity directly (SAS). So the converse of power of a point is equivalent to this SAS similarity.
