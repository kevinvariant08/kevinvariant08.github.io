About the monograph

In competitive mathematics, geometry is often perceived by students as a chaotic collection of messy intersections and phantom points, where auxiliary lines and constructions occur purely by luck. Beyond the Incenter dismantles this misconception, presenting synthetic geometry as a rigorous, systematic, and highly structured discipline.

Authored for the ambitious student bridging the gap between standard high school curricula and elite-level competitions, this textbook synthesizes classical theorems with modern methods. From the foundational properties of the triangle's five centers to advanced topics like projective geometry and spiral similarity, the text guides readers through the exact techniques required to conquer IMO-caliber configurations.

Why I wrote this book: the geometry gap

Having analysed UKMT markers' reports from the last decade, something alarms me: geometry is consistently the least-attempted and most poorly answered section. The markers explicitly note that geometry questions are "one of the least attempted by candidates," and constantly complain that students rely on "algebraic proof with no reference to the geometrical justifications."

The reason for this is simple: modern high school syllabuses have largely stripped out classical geometry. I see this at my own school. Out of over 1,000 students, only three know Menelaus's Theorem, and exactly two know how to use a complete quadrilateral. Because students lack these core geometric tools, they default to Cartesian coordinate or trigonometric bashes in competitions. As the markers point out, this usually leads to pages of algebra, which are highly prone to errors.

I wrote Beyond the Incenter to fix this specific gap. There are very few resources that bridge the gap from A-Level math to that elite standard. The goal of this book is to show that Olympiad geometry isn't about guessing "lucky" lines. By breaking down methods like projective geometry, radical axes, and spiral similarity, it provides students with a reliable, systematic approach to problems that otherwise look impossible.

TMUA mock papers

Full-length original mocks written to the real format: 20 multiple-choice questions in 75 minutes, no calculator. Every question is pitched at the level of the closing third of a genuine TMUA paper, so there is no warm-up ramp. Sit them under strict timed conditions and mark afterwards.

Set B · Papers 1 and 2 (newest)

The hardest set on this site. No routine questions: each one turns on a method or a trap that a standard A-level or Further Maths course does not teach. Paper 1 includes Chebyshev-style root permutations, Ptolemy on a heptagon, polynomial interpolation of an exponential, telescoping against a geometric factor, and definite integrals that are areas rather than antiderivatives. Paper 2 is reasoning at the olympiad end of the scale: invariants, counting two ways, remainders, pigeonhole, a subtraction game, colourings, functional equations, and a sign-change argument for integrals, every one a fresh instance rather than a known problem. Solutions are separate downloads so each paper can be sat cold.
