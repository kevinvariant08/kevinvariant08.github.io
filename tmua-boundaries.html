The official scale since 2024

Since UAT-UK took over the TMUA in 2024, each candidate gets one score from 1.0 to 9.0, combining both papers. Every question is worth one mark, with no penalty for a wrong answer. Each sitting is equated statistically, so there is no fixed raw-mark table, and UAT-UK publishes no grade boundaries or pass mark. The scale is anchored so that, in the October sitting, the median candidate scores 4.5 and the candidate at the 90th percentile scores 7.0.

  • 4.5median, October 2024 and October 2025
  • 3.325th percentile, both Octobers
  • 5.7–5.875th percentile (2024, 2025)
  • 7.090th percentile, both Octobers
  • 8,987 / 9,685October candidates in 2024 / 2025

January sittings score lower on average, so across a whole cycle the median is nearer 4.0. On a paper of official difficulty, JZMaths estimates that 4.5 needs about 17–19 marks out of 40, 6.5 about 25–27, and 7.5 about 30–32. It describes 6.5 and above as competitive for top courses.

How these boundaries were made

  1. One scale for questions and candidates. A question rated r on this site's 4.5 to 9.0 scale is taken to be solved, without guessing, by half of the candidates who would score r on the real TMUA.
  2. A response model. The chance of a correct answer rises smoothly as the candidate's score passes the question's rating (a logistic curve, as in the Rasch model UAT-UK uses). It never drops below 1 in the number of options, because blind guessing costs nothing.
  3. Calibration. The curve's steepness is fitted so that a paper of official difficulty reproduces the published raw-mark anchors above to within half a mark. As a check, the fit puts official TMUA questions at about 5.9 on this site's scale. That makes Sets D, E and F (average 5.6 to 5.8) close to the real thing, and Set C (6.4) harder, as its description says. That agreement was not built in.
  4. Boundaries. For every score from 1.0 to 9.0, the model gives the raw mark a candidate at that level is expected to get on each set, using each question's real rating and number of options. The table reads that backwards.

How much to trust them. Treat each estimate as plus or minus about 0.4. The ratings are expert judgements, not measured from thousands of candidates, and the anchors are estimates for the 2024+ scale. Drill shows a single-paper estimate, which assumes you would do equally well on the other paper. Anyone can rerun or adjust the model: tools/calibrate_boundaries.py in the site's repository.
