Nim Duel

You move first. On your turn, take any number of stones from one heap: tap a stone and it goes, together with every stone above it. Whoever takes the last stone wins. The bot never makes a mistake, but every position you start in can be won.

Why the bot wins (until you know this). Write each heap size in binary and add the columns without carrying: this is the nim-sum, written a ⊕ b ⊕ c. A position with nim-sum 0 is lost for the player about to move. Any move from such a position changes exactly one heap, so it changes some binary column and makes the nim-sum non-zero. And from a position with non-zero nim-sum there is always a move back to 0: take the highest column where the nim-sum has a 1, choose a heap with a 1 in that column, and shrink it to (that heap ⊕ nim-sum), which is smaller. So the winning strategy is to hand your opponent nim-sum 0 every time. This is Bouton's theorem (1901), and it is the seed of the Sprague–Grundy theory that solves every game of this kind.
